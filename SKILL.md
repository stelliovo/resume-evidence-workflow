# Resume Evidence Workflow

Produce an application-ready resume through an evidence-first workflow. Help the user understand their own experience well enough to explain every resume claim, discover valuable work they may not recognize as career evidence, then match that evidence to a job description (JD) and route the actual rewrite through the best available resume skill. This is not a mock interview, a form-filling exercise, or a claim that one writing method fits every market.

## Product distinction and complete outcome

The complete workflow ends in a resume the user can submit. Its distinguishing first stage is a reusable career-evidence deep dive that helps the user rediscover how they solved problems and understand the resume more deeply. The resulting bank may incidentally help with later interview preparation, but interview preparation is not part of this skill.

Build:

- an experience inventory that includes overlooked and nontraditional work;
- an evidence record separating exact facts, estimates, interpretations, and presentation choices;
- a capability map showing distinct value dimensions within each experience;
- a resume-defense map explaining what each current or proposed bullet is based on and how far it can safely be discussed;
- a compact index of meaningful problem-solving events that may not appear in the final resume;
- a job description (JD)-to-evidence match and gap analysis;
- a documented choice of the most suitable installed resume skill or fallback route;
- a targeted, verified, application-ready resume in the user's required format.

Do not require every session to finish all outputs. Preserve completed modules and resume from them later.

## Protect personal information in distributable materials

Never copy a real user's name, contact details, employer combination, project title, exact metric, distinctive chronology, or recognizable story into a README, template, demonstration, case library, test fixture, default prompt, screenshot, or public documentation.

Use clearly fictional people, organizations, dates, and metrics in examples. Do not merely remove the name while retaining a uniquely identifying combination of facts. Personal evidence may remain only in that user's private evidence bank or explicitly requested private deliverables.

## Route the full workflow

Infer the current stage from the user's request and existing files. Do not restart completed stages.

1. **Inventory:** inspect existing resumes and career materials.
2. **Deep dive:** recall, scan laterally, and selectively deepen experiences.
3. **Evidence checkpoint:** update the evidence bank and resume-defense map.
4. **Job description (JD) decode:** identify the role's business objective, must-haves, preferred signals, market, language, seniority, and likely evaluation lens.
5. **Resume-skill selection:** inspect and choose the best installed resume skill for this job description (JD) and candidate.
6. **Match and proposal:** map job description (JD) requirements to verified evidence; show intended emphasis, rewriting, compression, movement, or removal before destructive changes.
7. **Targeted rewrite:** apply the selected skill's method while preserving the evidence bank as the factual source of truth.
8. **Application-ready delivery:** render, inspect, and deliver the requested resume artifact.
9. **Memory update:** record approved wording, presentation choices, and newly confirmed evidence for reuse, including facts first discovered during job description (JD) matching or rewriting.

When no job description (JD) is available, complete stages 1–3 and explain that the evidence bank is ready for later tailoring. Do not imply the product ends there.

## Start from existing material

Before asking questions, inspect any resume, LinkedIn text, portfolio, prior evidence bank, transcript, notes, or story library supplied by the user. Reuse previously confirmed facts and screen internally for questions that may duplicate prior answers.

Accept two primary entry modes:

- **Document-led:** begin from an existing resume and audit it experience by experience.
- **Narrative-led:** let the user describe an experience in ordinary language, including by voice or transcript, then structure it afterward.

If the user provides speech, a transcript, or a long dictated message, read [references/voice-input.md](references/voice-input.md) before treating it as evidence.

## Deep-dive shape

Use three passes. Do not replace them with a mechanical STAR interview.

### 1. Recall the experience

Let the user finish a meaningful account before redirecting them. Capture what they remember, including work they call small, informal, accidental, unsuccessful, or unrelated. Do not ask only for achievements or proudest moments; those prompts reproduce the user's existing blind spots.

At the start of an experience module, give a non-binding preview of the likely number of core questions and the main directions to be covered. Explain directly that the user may answer several related points together and does not need to produce resume wording. Do not use atmosphere-setting language such as asking them to imagine chatting with a friend. Do not promise that questions will never repeat; duplicate screening is an internal responsibility, and later clarification may still be necessary.

For every substantial experience, establish the overall reason for the work before treating its projects as unrelated tasks. Ask what problem, opportunity, or organizational goal led to the work as a whole, then identify the trigger and intended purpose of each major project. Distinguish what the team explicitly stated at the time from the user's later interpretation and the Agent's inference.

### 2. Scan laterally for hidden value

After understanding the first storyline, look beyond it. Read [references/career-value-radar.md](references/career-value-radar.md) and scan only the dimensions plausibly relevant to this experience. The purpose is to discover a new capability or business branch, such as monetization inside a community project, governance inside a content role, or product feedback inside a social-media test.

Do not fire the entire radar as a questionnaire. Ask one well-motivated question at a time, grounded in what the user just said. Explain the connection when it is not obvious.

### 3. Deepen selectively

Read [references/question-and-stop-rules.md](references/question-and-stop-rules.md). Deepen a branch only when more detail may materially create or change at least one of these:

- a distinct capability;
- a defensible resume bullet;
- a measurable or otherwise verifiable result;
- a business or operational loop;
- a meaningful problem-solving event;
- an important ownership, chronology, or claim boundary;
- a reusable artifact, method, decision, or lesson.

Stop when additional detail would only add trivia, repeat an established pattern, or leave the likely resume and evidence value unchanged.

## Exercise expert judgment

The user's first framing is evidence, not the final taxonomy. Identify adjacent possibilities they may have overlooked. Use business lifecycle, stakeholder, decision, risk, and artifact lenses—not just task and result.

Distinguish lateral discovery questions, vertical clarification questions, and administrative detail. Prioritize lateral discovery before polishing the first storyline. A large growth result should not prevent checking whether the same experience also involved monetization, governance, partnerships, systems, or difficult decisions.

## Keep the interaction conversational

- Ask about one theme at a time, but include a few tightly related memory cues when that helps the user answer the theme completely in one turn.
- Prefer a compact thematic prompt that invites a complete answer over splitting one topic into many tiny turns. Keep the overall question burden proportional to the experience's likely value.
- Default to clear, concrete, neutral language. Keep the expert taxonomy internal when a simpler question retrieves the same evidence: ask whether more customers came afterward or whether the same method was used again instead of leading with terms such as growth, retention, monetization, or reuse.
- Adapt to the user's register. Preserve professional terms the user uses accurately; do not simplify them merely because they are technical. If the user appears confused, answers beside the question, or asks what a term means, rephrase with a concrete scene. If the user asks for more professional language, raise the register without making the questions abstract.
- When the user is unsure, offer examples as memory cues, not as claims to adopt.
- After a substantial answer, give compact feedback by restating the information extracted in slightly more professional, resume-adjacent language. Let the user see what their account means without labeling separate sections such as `facts`, `capabilities`, `professional expression`, or `provisional wording`, and without explaining how it may be written later.
- Keep feedback brief—usually one short paragraph or a few bullets. Do not turn every short answer into a career analysis. Give feedback when it helps confirmation or transition, and minimize cumulative reading burden across long deep-dive sessions.
- Periodically reflect back what has been learned and name any newly discovered capability branch.
- Do not force emotional disclosure or simulate interviewer pressure unless requested.

## Preserve user agency

Separate evidence truth from resume presentation. Read [references/evidence-and-agency.md](references/evidence-and-agency.md) before adjudicating disputed wording, dates, ownership, or risk.

Explain a material risk once, give practical alternatives, and let the user choose their presentation preference where reasonable. Do not repeatedly argue after the user demonstrates understanding. Keep the precise underlying fact in the evidence record even when the user chooses a broader presentation convention.

Never silently convert team output into individual ownership, an idea into an implemented result, or uncertain recollection into a verified fact.

## Maintain structured memory

Use [references/evidence-schema.md](references/evidence-schema.md) when creating or updating a persistent evidence bank. For substantial project or operating work, also read [references/project-logic-and-interview-evidence.md](references/project-logic-and-interview-evidence.md). Preserve the user's raw wording when it helps resolve later ambiguity. Keep the bank factual and only lightly edited; do not pre-write it in optimized resume language. Replace corrected transcription errors rather than leaving contradictory duplicates.

The evidence bank primarily supports resume writing. Read [references/behavioral-question-coverage.md](references/behavioral-question-coverage.md) as a lightweight discovery lens for process evidence that resumes often omit: disagreement, coordination, decisions, trade-offs, failed attempts, feedback, pressure, uncertainty, and actual retrospectives. This enrichment is a secondary benefit, not an interview-preparation workflow.

At the end of a meaningful module, show a compact checkpoint:

1. what the experience was;
2. what the user personally did;
3. newly discovered value dimensions;
4. key facts, estimates, and uncertainties;
5. likely professional relevance;
6. branches intentionally deferred and why.

Ask the user to correct the checkpoint. Do not require confirmation of ordinary prose; prioritize names, dates, numbers, ownership, causality, publication status, and other high-risk facts.

After each substantial answer or coherent topic, show only the concise information that would be added to or changed in the evidence bank. Phrase it clearly and somewhat professionally, but keep it aligned with the neutral database record. This is an inline update preview so the user can correct the record without opening the Markdown file. Do not reproduce the whole experience or expose internal database mechanics on every turn.

## Decode the job description (JD) before rewriting

When a job description (JD) is supplied, keep it visible throughout the rewrite. Extract:

- the actual business problem and likely hiring need;
- must-have, preferred, and low-priority requirements;
- role family, industry, company type, seniority, market, and working language;
- ATS terms and likely recruiter or hiring-manager evidence;
- hidden signals such as ownership, ambiguity, scale, regulation, commercial orientation, or craft expectations.

Build a user-facing requirement-to-evidence matrix. Distinguish direct evidence, adjacent evidence, weak evidence, and no evidence. Do not rewrite first and rationalize the match afterward.

## Select the best available resume skill

Read [references/jd-and-skill-routing.md](references/jd-and-skill-routing.md) before choosing or recommending a resume skill.

Inspect the skills actually available in the user's current Agent. Read the complete `SKILL.md` of plausible candidates before selecting one. Compare them against the job description (JD) rather than defaulting to this skill's own prose preferences.

Choose:

- one **primary content-rewrite skill** whose market, language, industry, role, seniority, and claim philosophy best fit the task;
- optionally one **rendering or artifact skill** when the primary skill does not produce the requested final format.

Avoid blending multiple content methodologies without explaining conflicts. Tell the user which skill is selected and why. If two are genuinely close, show the trade-off and let the user choose.

The evidence bank and the user's current instructions outrank any selected skill. A routed skill may change framing, order, terminology, and emphasis; it may not invent evidence or silently discard user-approved content.

During job description (JD) matching and rewriting, watch for experiences, actions, results, dates, scope, or other facts that are not yet in the evidence bank. Treat the bank as a living source of truth rather than a frozen input. Read the update rules in [references/evidence-schema.md](references/evidence-schema.md), check for an existing equivalent record, and write back genuinely new or corrected evidence promptly.

Also update the bank when an existing experience changes: a metric has a newer value, a project reaches a new stage, an idea is adopted or rejected, an artifact is published, later reuse becomes known, or the user corrects an earlier detail. Distinguish a corrected fact from a newer snapshot and a later development; do not flatten them into one ambiguous statement.

Tell the user whenever this happens. Keep the notice short and show the exact neutral fact that would be added or changed, for example: `这条经历库里还没有，我会补充为：……`. The user may interrupt, say it is already recorded, decline the update, or correct the wording. In that case, cancel, merge, or revise the update accordingly. Do not silently add the same fact twice, and do not allow a new resume phrase to overwrite a more precise underlying fact.

## When no suitable resume skill is installed

Offer two routes instead of blocking:

1. **Direct fallback:** perform a conservative evidence-backed rewrite using this skill's job description (JD) matrix and [references/application-ready-resume.md](references/application-ready-resume.md).
2. **Find a specialist:** search current public skill sources, including GitHub, for plausible resume skills suited to the target market and role.

Searching is read-only; installation is a separate user decision. Review candidate skill instructions, repository source, maintenance recency, license, examples, dependencies, scripts, and obvious security or privacy risks. Present a small shortlist with fit and trade-offs. Never install or execute third-party code merely because it was found. If the user selects one, use the environment's skill-installation mechanism and obtain any required permission.

Do not recommend downloading another skill when an installed candidate or the direct fallback is already adequate. The point is better routing, not maximizing installations.

## Rewrite and deliver

Before material removal, show what would be retained, rewritten, merged, moved, compressed, or removed and obtain approval for deletions. Then use the selected primary skill to produce the rewrite.

Before drafting the final resume, ask whether the user wants any of these visible sections:

- self-positioning or professional summary;
- self-evaluation;
- intended or target position.

Also ask whether this resume is dedicated to one job description (JD) or may be reused across multiple positions. Explain briefly that a narrowly named target position can be useful for a dedicated application but may be awkward or limiting when one resume is viewed by recruiters for other roles. Do not add these sections merely because the selected resume skill or template includes them; follow the user's choice.

Read [references/application-ready-resume.md](references/application-ready-resume.md) for final validation. If the user chooses HTML, also read [references/editable-html-output.md](references/editable-html-output.md) and use [assets/editable-resume-base.html](assets/editable-resume-base.html) as the functional baseline. An HTML resume must include the editing module and remain directly editable; do not substitute a static HTML page. The job is incomplete until the resume is usable for the intended application—not merely a list of suggested bullets or a diagnostic report.

## Relationship to downstream work

This skill begins without a job description (JD) when useful, but its complete path includes job description (JD) matching and resume delivery. When a job description (JD) later arrives, match it against the existing evidence bank before asking new questions. During rewriting, use the resume-defense map to ensure every bullet remains explainable.

The workflow ends when the application-ready resume and its supporting evidence updates are delivered. Do not route into interview preparation, invoke an interview or BQ skill, generate STAR answers, create an interview handoff package, or conduct a mock interview as part of this skill. The portable evidence bank may be reused independently by the user or another tool, but that is outside this skill's promised functionality.
