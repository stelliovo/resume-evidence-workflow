# Behavioral-Signal Awareness

Use this only as an internal discovery lens after the core business facts of an experience are clear. Its purpose is to preserve useful process evidence that a result-focused resume may omit, not to conduct interview preparation or complete a competency checklist.

## High-value event signals

Look only for signals plausibly present in the experience:

- disagreement, cross-team difficulty, or persuasion without authority;
- an unclear problem, incomplete information, or a consequential decision;
- failure, error, negative feedback, or a changed approach;
- time, budget, staffing, or priority constraints;
- initiative, ownership, improvement, or helping others proceed;
- customer conflict, sensitive risk, or a difficult trade-off;
- an actual retrospective and changes applied afterward.

Ask at most one broad, natural event question per experience unless a meaningful event emerges. For example: “这件事推进时，有没有哪次大家想法不一样、条件突然变了，或者第一次做法没有效果，最后需要你重新处理？” Adapt the examples to the user's actual context and do not imply that any event occurred.

If an event appears, preserve only:

- what made the situation difficult;
- the user's responsibility and decision;
- what they actually did;
- the direct outcome;
- whether the team later reviewed or changed anything.

Keep one to three strong events per substantial experience, not one per category. If no event emerges, stop. Do not invoke a BQ skill, generate STAR/CAR answers, predict questions, or build an interview handoff package within this workflow.
